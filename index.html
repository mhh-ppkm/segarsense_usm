Printable label

The label has a window for the indicator film, white and grey patches for camera calibration, and three reference colours so shoppers can judge freshness by eye even without the app.

Label settings

Print at 60 × 40 mm on matte white paper to reduce glare. The indicator film sits inside the pack facing the headspace, and the label window must be transparent.

Experimental workflow

  1. Prepare the indicator film (for example chitosan/PVA or starch with butterfly pea or roselle anthocyanin, or curcumin) and test its response in pH 2–12 buffer solutions.
  2. Pack chicken fillets with the film in the pack headspace. Store one set chilled (e.g. 4 °C) and one at room temperature for accelerated testing.
  3. Every day, scan the label with the app and measure TVB-N, pH and TPC at the same time. Save each reading with its true label based on your chosen TVB-N limits.
  4. Photograph with several phones and lighting conditions. This builds a model that copes with real supermarket variation.
  5. Check the Data & model tab: leave-one-out accuracy, confusion matrix and R² of the TVB-N prediction. Once satisfactory, the app classifies new scans with that model.

How the app works

Pixel colour is converted from sRGB to CIELAB (D65) after white-balance correction using the white patch. Colour difference is measured with the CIEDE2000 formula (ΔE₀₀). When at least two classes have two labelled readings each for the selected dye, the app uses a weighted k-nearest-neighbour (kNN) classifier. Otherwise it uses the dye’s preset reference colours, and if the batch has an earliest-day reading, ΔE₀₀ against that day is shown too. TVB-N is predicted by multiple linear regression on a* and b* once at least four readings have TVB-N values.

Preset reference colours are approximations from the literature. For publishable results, replace them with calibration data from your own film.
